Developer profile: making credibility measurable

Helping homebuyers look beyond a developer’s name by showing how their completed projects have appreciated over time.

In development

Role
Principal Product Designer
Platform
Web (Desktop + Mobile), Android
Company
Housing.com
Context

A homebuyer evaluating a ₹1–3 Cr property may wonder, “What has this developer delivered before, and has it created value over time?”

A developer’s past performance can be a powerful signal of what buyers can expect from their next project. This experience brings that track record into the buying journey, helping users evaluate projects with greater context and confidence.

01 Problem

Buyers had no clear way to evaluate a developer’s track record before enquiring about a project.

This raised a larger question: how could Housing.com add meaningful evidence to a high-consideration decision?

  • Has the developer created value before? Past projects lacked performance context.
  • Can performance build trust? Brand alone wasn’t enough for lesser-known developers.
  • Why look elsewhere? Buyers had to research the track record outside Housing.com.
Problem space

Why the old page held it back

01 Thin context, weak decision support

The previous developer SRP surfaced only basic facts like name, establishment year, and project count, giving buyers little to assess long-term value or credibility.

02 Project cards without a reason to stay

Beyond the cards, the page offered little decision-making context, making it feel like another SRP rather than a destination that helped users evaluate a developer.

02 The solution

Built the page to be a credibility-first Developer SRP that earns trust through verifiable signals.

I redesigned the Developer SRP to help buyers evaluate developers through independently verifiable factors like price appreciation, credibility signals, and transparent project history, while keeping the experience easy to scan and explore.

Two tabs, each with a clear job

I split the page into separate tabs: a scannable project list, and the developer’s profile information. This kept the developer info from getting lost between projects and brought down the length of the page significantly. Switch between the tabs below to see what each one does.

Users land here by default, because viewing property is the core action on Housing.com.

03 What I did

A credibility-first approach to a trust-building experience

Each design decision was tested against one question: does this help a buyer verify, or does it just reassure?

The design lens

I designed against the brochure, not alongside it.

Every developer’s own site promises trust. The aggregator’s edge was that it didn’t have to. The design question was never what should we say about this developer? It was what can we actually prove?

The developer’s claim Unverified

“Trust us. 10,000 happy families.”

The platform’s advantage Verifiable
  • What price data shows over time.
  • Verified RERA and possession status.
The buyer’s question

Do I have enough evidence — not reassurance — to take the next step?

01

Competitor analysis

The market had plenty of developer pages. What it lacked was useful decision-making context. Studying 99acres, Magicbricks, Godrej, Adani and M3M revealed a clear gap in how developers were presented.

Key takeaway

The opportunity wasn’t more information. It was more independent evidence.

Housing.com could use its position as an aggregator to give buyers context developers couldn’t provide themselves.

01 The usual developer page

Logo, experience, project count, locations.

Basic facts, little decision support.

02 What stood out on 99acres
  • Price appreciation
  • Resident reviews
  • Peer comparisons

Signals buyers could actually evaluate.

03 What developer sites couldn’t offer

Self-reported claims could describe a developer, but couldn’t objectively validate them.

02

Design challenges while iterating wireframes

To explore more directions and speed up the process, I used Claude to generate the wireframes.

The roadblocks I hit while exploring wireframes & iterations were:

Challenge 01

Projects first, not Insights

Insights gives the most complete view of a developer, so it was a strong case for the first tab.

We still opened on Projects, because viewing property is the core action on Housing.com. Insights is one tap away.

Challenge 02

Including social proof

Social proof builds trust, but we weren’t confident we had enough data to back it.

After stakeholder discussions, we left it out rather than dilute the page’s quality.

Challenge 03

One long page, or two tabs

Early iterations were a single page. As it grew longer, the projects list lost the focus it needed.

So I split it into two tabs, keeping Projects centre stage, as in the final design.

04 The result

A developer profile built for better decisions

Project history, appreciation data and credibility signals now give buyers a clearer view of a developer’s track record.

The result: a more useful, consistent developer experience on Housing.com.

Still in development, so impact can’t be measured yet. Results will follow after launch.

Reflection

Proof over promises

Buyers didn’t need more claims about a developer. They needed evidence they could check, without it slowing down their search.

01 Evidence beats reassurance

Appreciation data says more than a developer’s own claims, because the platform has nothing to sell.

02 Leave out what you can’t back

Social proof would have built trust, but thin data would have weakened it. Holding it back kept the page credible.

03 Keep the core action centre stage

Two tabs gave credibility its own space without pushing projects down the page.

What’s next

From a profile page to a trust layer

The next phase adds the signals we held back and tests the bets we made.
